HW 3: Unity Interactive Environment

CS 480 · Unity Interactive Assignment

Gameplay Demo

My Environment

For this assignment, I created a custom interactive living room and family environment in unity. It features open-source models including those of the player's family memobers. The players brother, father, mother and family dog all feature interactions triggered when approached. The result is a kind of chaotic scene of silly interactions. The brother does not like being touched and immediately dramatically falls to the ground when approached. The mother offers the player a comically large apple as a "light snack", which the player can also "eat" by approaching it. A sound effect also insues. The father has seemingly been cured of his condition, standing victoriously by his wheelchair. The dog obviously wants to be fed, barking when approached. A translation tells the player to feed the dog, if he dares. Overall this is the average weekend morning in this fictional family's home.

Key takeaways

I found that for an environment to be believable at all, there must be a lot of padding content - a lot of objects and sounds which hold no plot significance but immerse the user. This is rather time consuming and tedious.

Secondly, I struggled with sizing the the different models as well as their collider boxes. Certain models were incompatible altogether, and I needed to find replacements. There is also a lot of trial and error required to place and size the colliders naturally within the environment.

Finally, the initial position and direction of the camera has a significant impact on the player's experience.

Challenges

One of the main challenges I faced was ensuring all the different elements of the environment worked together seamlessly. This included managing the interactions between various objects and making sure the player could navigate the space effectively. Another surprising challenge was managing the brother's built-in model animation. I found it unintuitive to work with but I managed. In general, it's also difficult to estimate the spacing of elements in 3D space represented on a 2D screen.
